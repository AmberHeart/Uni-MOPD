Where should a multi-teacher distillation student start?

Base, Multi-Domain SFT, merges and the strongest single specialist under the same MOPD run, read benchmark by benchmark. A training-free uniform merge starts where the base model ends after 200 updates, and the starts differ far less after 50 updates than before.

Most MOPD work starts the student from the base model or a multi-domain SFT checkpoint and leaves much of the integration to distillation. When the specialists share a base model, merging them first is a better start: the merge scores 67.6 on our 19-benchmark average before any training, within 0.1 points of what the base model reaches after 200 distillation updates. Among strong starts, a higher initial score does not give a better final model, and no start wins on most benchmarks.

A training-free merge starts where Base ends

Every start gets the same distillation run: same queries, same five teachers, same full-vocabulary reverse KL. Only the starting weights change.

Tuned merge 68.5 69.8 69.4
OCR specialist 68.4 69.7 —

The best-specialist reference is a hypothetical model that matches the strongest specialist in every domain. The merge covers 74% of the distance from the base model to it before training, and 92% after 50 updates.

Spiky specialists, flat merge

A specialist leads its own domain and can fall far behind in others, which domain averages hide. The VQA specialist scores 17.6 on DocVQA, where every other model is above 91, and the STEM specialist scores 1.5 on ScreenSpot-Pro.

The merge flattens the spikes. On 15 of 19 benchmarks it reaches at least 90% of the best specialist’s score, and on OCRBench v2, ScreenSpot-v2, EmbSpatial-Bench, MMStar and DocVQA it is within 1% or above. The remaining gaps concentrate on four benchmarks: BabyVision (15.2 against 22.4), We-Math (64.4 against 77.0), RefSpatial (60.3 against 70.4) and CharXiv (57.2 against 65.8). We-Math, RefSpatial and CharXiv then receive the largest gains in the first 50 updates (next note); BabyVision gains 2.1 and keeps most of its gap. Merging needs a shared base model and vocabulary, so that the task vectors (specialist weights minus base weights) add up into a usable model.

Nineteen benchmarks, four starts

  • The strong starts interleave. Across Multi-Domain SFT, the uniform merge and the tuned merge, the mean per-benchmark gap between the best and the worst start is 3.0 points before MOPD, 1.4 after 50 updates and 1.3 after 200. At update 200 the best start differs by benchmark: Multi-Domain SFT leads on nine, the tuned merge on five, the uniform merge on four, Base on one.
  • Base closes most of its distance within 50 updates, but not all. At update 50 it is still below all three strong starts on 15 of the 19 benchmarks, and trails the uniform merge by 11.0 points on CharXiv, 8.7 on RefSpatial, 7.6 on We-Math and 5.8 on MUIRBench.
  • The OCR benchmarks dip. For the uniform merge, DocVQA and OCRBench v2 fall to 93.4 and 63.8 at update 50 and recover only partly by update 200 (94.4 and 65.8); the tuned merge and Multi-Domain SFT dip in the same direction. The OCR domain mean stays flat (77.0 before, 77.8 after 50 updates) because CharXiv rises by 7.0.

The gap between the strong starts closes as soon as MOPD begins and stays closed.

Scope.

Single seed per start; a domain mean varies by up to 0.7 between evaluations of the same checkpoint.

Starts differ before MOPD, and much less after

Before MOPD every start has a distinct profile. The OCR specialist leads on We-Math by 11.0 and CharXiv by 8.6 and trails on RefSpatial by 3.6; the tuned merge leads on ScreenSpot-Pro, OSWorld-G, MathVision and CharXiv by 3.4 to 3.8; the calibrated PGD merges give up about 10 points on MMMU-Pro and CharXiv (coefficient calibration). After 50 updates the large differences are gone: 64 of the 95 differences are within 2 points before MOPD and 75 of 95 afterwards, and the largest shrinks from 18.2 to 4.9. What remains is tied to the start: Multi-Domain SFT and the OCR start still trail the merge on RefSpatial (by 3.6) and MMSI-Bench (by 2.6 and 2.5), and the PGD merges trail on MUIRBench and TreeBench.

Strong, not optimal

The tuned merge starts 0.9 points ahead of the uniform merge and ends level with it, and Multi-Domain SFT starts 1.8 points behind and needs more than twice as many updates to reach the merge’s update-50 score. The strongest single specialist, the OCR specialist at 68.4, first loses ground: its STEM mean falls from 71.5 to 66.4 by update 20, and it reaches 69.7 only at update 50, while the merge rises steadily to 69.8.

The same order holds under domain- and source-balanced sampling (69.3 for the OCR start, 69.5 for the merge). One possible reading is that the merge already sits near a compromise between the five teachers, whereas a single specialist sits at one corner, with four teachers pulling at once.

The score before MOPD therefore works as a filter, not a ranking. It separates strong starts from Base, which is 12.8 points below the reference, but says little about the endpoint among strong starts. What differs is how fast they get there.

A second specialist bank

Scope.

Four specialists, 16 benchmarks, and an average over four domain means. These numbers are not comparable with the 19-benchmark, five-domain average used elsewhere on this site.

The OCR and GUI specialists of the main bank are broad and the two strongest overall. A second bank removes them: it keeps the STEM, VQA and embodied specialists, adds a narrower OCR specialist and drops GUI.

The pattern carries over. The merge scores 65.0 without training, the level the base model reaches after 200 updates. Both merges peak at update 100 (67.6 and 67.7), within 0.3 points of the 67.9 that the STEM start reaches only at update 200. Scores saturate around update 100 here rather than 50, so the budget should be read off held-out scores on a new bank.

Two details are specific to this bank. The score dips coincide with long-response failures, which the merges resolve early: on AI2D their truncation rate peaks at 23–24% at update 25 and falls to about 5% by update 50, while the STEM start’s rate climbs to 41% and 76% at updates 100 and 150.

The embodied mean reaches 55.0 after 50 updates and 56.0 at update 100 (56.2 at scale 1.25), level with its specialist’s 56.0. In the main bank, where other specialists are strong on part of the embodied domain, the unified model passes its specialist (57.1).

In practice

  • If your specialists share a base model, merge them with uniform weights before distilling; it costs no training.
  • Don’t rank candidate starts by their score before distillation.
  • Compare starts after a short run at your planned budget, and look beyond the average: starts differ benchmark by benchmark.
