What does distillation add to a strong start?

Fifty MOPD updates recover 67% of the gap the merge leaves. The gains go where the domain teacher still leads, arrive on different benchmarks at different times, and embodied understanding ends above its own specialist. The loss keeps falling long after the scores stop.

The merge already integrates most of what the specialists know and leaves a set of specific gaps. Fifty MOPD updates take the uniform merge from 67.6 to 69.8, raise every domain, and close 67% of what the merge left. This note looks at where the gains land, when they arrive, and why the loss is a poor guide to them.

Gains go where the teacher still leads

Across the 19 benchmarks, the gain after 50 updates tracks how far the domain teacher still leads the merge (Spearman ρ = 0.77).

The largest gains are where the teacher led most: We-Math gains 7.6 against a 12.6-point gap, RefSpatial 7.9 against 10.1, CharXiv 7.0 against 8.6. On DocVQA and OCRBench v2 the teacher leads the merge by less than a point or trails it, and little remains to transfer. Routing is the likely reason: each query goes to its domain’s teacher, so supervision is strongest where that teacher still knows more than the student. It also explains why more elaborate sampling has little left to add (see domain sampling).

When each benchmark moves

  • Gains do not arrive together. We-Math rises by 4.2 in the first 20 updates and reaches +7.6 at update 50. MUIRBench stays within about one point of the merge until update 40, then jumps to +5.8 at update 50 and holds between +5.5 and +6.6 through update 200.
  • Some gains are partly given back. We-Math falls to +2.6 at update 75 and climbs to +5.5 by update 200. RefSpatial peaks at +10.1 at update 75 and ends at +6.1, CharXiv peaks at +8.0 and ends at +5.0. TreeBench keeps rising, from +2.2 at update 50 to +4.2 at update 175.
  • Some benchmarks decline. OCRBench v2 and DocVQA fall below the merge in the first updates and recover only partly (−3.8 and −1.4 at update 50, −1.7 and −0.4 at update 200). EmbSpatial-Bench slips by 1.7 by update 200.
  • Several benchmarks barely move. ScreenSpot-v2, ScreenSpot-Pro, DynaMath and MMBench stay within about 1.5 points of the merge at every checkpoint, so the average gain comes from the other fifteen.

The average is flat from update 75 to 200 (69.4 to 69.7, against 69.8 at update 50) while individual benchmarks still move by several points, so the pattern across benchmarks is more stable than any single checkpoint.

One domain passes its own specialist

The embodied specialist scores 56.0 on its domain. The unified model reaches 57.1 and stays at or above 56.0 from update 30 on. The two stages supply different halves: the embodied specialist leads only on referring and multi-image spatial reasoning, while on TreeBench and EmbSpatial-Bench the VQA and OCR specialists are stronger and the merge keeps their strengths. MOPD then recovers most of the RefSpatial gap, from 60.3 to 68.2, the largest one left in the domain.

Scope.

Main run, one seed, first 50 updates, measured during training.

The training logs agree. Embodied is where the merged student starts furthest from its teacher in distribution, and its agreement rises most, until it is level with the GUI and VQA domains.

The loss keeps falling after the score stops

The training loss measures agreement with the teacher on the student’s own outputs. Averaged over all domains, it falls from 0.32 in the first 10 updates to 0.11 in updates 41 to 50 and 0.05 in updates 191 to 200, while the average score levels at update 50.

Loss and score relate differently across domains. In embodied and STEM the loss falls (1.07 to 0.27 and 0.11 to 0.05) as the score rises (54.4 to 57.1 and 67.4 to 71.0). In GUI the loss halves between the first and last window (0.28 to 0.14) while the score moves only from 78.0 to 78.3, because most of its gain, from 77.0, arrives in the first 10 updates. In VQA the loss edges up in the last window (0.250 to 0.259) while the score rises by 1.3.

Neither the loss nor the first few checkpoints is a reliable stopping rule; judge a run by a small held-out benchmark panel. Every checkpoint from update 75 to 200 stays within 0.5 points of update 50, which is why we use 50 updates.

The student has to generate

The same recipe trained on fixed prefixes written by the teachers reaches 68.6 after 50 updates, 1.2 points below the on-policy run (69.8), and trails at every checkpoint (67.6, 67.9 and 68.2 at updates 20, 30 and 40, against 68.3, 68.7 and 69.0). One plausible reason is that the student’s mistakes appear in its own responses rather than in the teacher’s, so only on-policy data shows the teacher where to correct.

In practice

  • Distill after merging, and budget tens of updates, not hundreds.
  • Expect the gains where the domain teacher still leads, and little where it does not, including small losses on benchmarks where the teacher is no better than the merge.
  • Keep the responses on-policy.
  • Stop on held-out scores, not on the loss.
