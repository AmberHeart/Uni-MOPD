Uni-MOPD / Research notes

What does the choice of distillation loss change?

Reverse KL, forward KL and ToDi end within 0.4 points of each other on the average, but they get there differently: forward KL and ToDi recover General VQA earlier, reverse KL sharpens the student in the embodied and GUI domains, and distilling on the sampled token alone loses a point and a half.

From a strong start, the divergence barely changes the final average: reverse KL and ToDi, an adaptive mix of forward and reverse KL, both reach 69.8 after 50 updates, and forward KL reaches 69.4. The benchmark scores and training logs show that the three nevertheless behave differently on the way there. What the student is supervised with matters more than the divergence: a policy gradient on the sampled token gains 0.5 points in 50 updates, while supervising a distribution gains 1.7 to 2.0.

Three divergences, same run

All three start from the uniform merge and share everything except the per-token divergence between the student distribution p and the teacher distribution q:

  • Reverse KL (RKL), DKL​(p∥q), penalizes student mass on tokens the teacher considers unlikely.
  • Forward KL (FKL), DKL​(q∥p), penalizes the student for missing tokens the teacher likes.
  • ToDi mixes the two per token, weighted by σ(logq−logp).
Divergence Avg. at 50 STEM VQA OCR GUI Embodied
RKL 69.8 71.0 64.9 77.8 78.3 57.1
ToDi 69.8 70.8 65.5 77.6 78.3 56.9
FKL 69.4 70.9 65.0 77.2 78.3 55.6

Forward KL and ToDi recover VQA earlier

The averages cross over: at update 30 ToDi (69.3) and FKL (69.0) are ahead of RKL (68.7), and by update 50 RKL has caught up with ToDi and passed FKL. The domains explain the crossover.

  • General VQA. From update 30, FKL and ToDi are ahead: 63.7 and 63.8 where RKL scores 62.3, and at update 40 FKL leads by 1.5. RKL catches up at update 50 (64.9 against 65.0 for FKL and 65.5 for ToDi). The order is mixed earlier, and FKL dips to 61.6 at update 10.
  • Embodied. The order reverses. RKL reaches 57.1 and keeps rising, ToDi reaches 56.9, and FKL stays between 55.0 and 55.6 from update 20 on.
  • OCR dips under all three. At update 10 it falls from 77.0 to 75.9 (RKL), 75.2 (FKL) and 75.3 (ToDi), and recovers to 77.2 to 77.8 by update 50. The dip does not depend on the divergence.
  • STEM and GUI end within 0.3 points of one another.

On most benchmarks the three divergences agree to within a point or two, and the differences concentrate on a few. MUIRBench gains 5.8 under RKL, 6.7 under FKL and 7.5 under ToDi; BabyVision gains 2.1, 2.8 and 4.4. On the embodied side, RefSpatial is where forward KL falls behind: it gains 2.5, against 7.9 for RKL and 9.0 for ToDi. RKL also gains more on CharXiv (7.0 against 4.5 for FKL and 5.9 for ToDi).

Scope.

One seed per divergence, one start, 50 updates; a domain mean varies by up to 0.7 between evaluations of the same checkpoint. The VQA gap at update 30 (1.4) and the embodied gap at update 50 (1.5) exceed that.

What the training logs show

Losses cannot be compared across divergences, since each run’s loss is its own divergence on its own scale. Two diagnostics are measured the same way in all three runs, on the student’s own responses: how often the student’s 16 most likely tokens are also among the teacher’s 16, and the entropy of the student’s distribution.

Forward KL keeps the student’s distribution broad: its entropy stays above RKL’s in every domain, most clearly in embodied (0.35 against 0.12 at update 50) and GUI (0.19 against 0.09). In these two domains it also does not raise agreement with the teacher. In embodied the overlap falls from 0.60 to 0.54 under FKL while it rises from 0.58 to 0.79 under RKL and to 0.75 under ToDi; GUI follows the same order (0.73 to 0.70 under FKL, 0.75 to 0.78 under RKL). ToDi sits between the two on both diagnostics.

Our reading is that a mass-covering loss spends part of its signal keeping every token the teacher likes alive. That suits benchmarks that draw on broad knowledge, where the student has to bring back more of what it knew, and costs the benchmarks where the right answer is one precise output, such as the referring tasks of RefSpatial; a mode-seeking loss does the opposite. ToDi’s per-token weighting interpolates: it recovers VQA about as early as forward KL and keeps most of reverse KL’s embodied gain. This interprets one run each.

Scope.

Window means of the first 50 updates; one seed per divergence. Overlap and entropy are measured on the student’s samples, which differ between runs.

The divergence matters more offline

The same divergences also fit merge coefficients offline (merge coefficients), where the choice moves the score before MOPD by −0.7 to +1.1 points. Online, from a strong start, the three end within 0.4 points: when the student generates its own responses and the teacher scores them, the divergence has less to decide.

How much of the teacher’s distribution do you need?

To vary only the per-token supervision, we reimplemented the method in verl and ran four variants from the uniform merge that differ only in the per-token objective:

  • Sampled token: a policy gradient on the token the student generated, with advantage logq(yt​)−logpθ​(yt​).
  • Student top-16 / top-128: the Open-MOPD surrogate over the student’s k most likely tokens.
  • Full vocabulary: the full reverse KL of our recipe.
Objective Avg. @50 Gain STEM VQA OCR GUI Emb.
Sampled token 68.1 +0.5 69.2 62.3 76.0 78.1 55.2
Student top-16 69.6 +2.0 70.6 64.9 77.5 78.6 56.5
Student top-128 69.6 +2.0 71.2 64.9 77.2 78.4 56.4
Full vocabulary 69.4 +1.7 70.3 64.2 77.8 78.5 56.0
Full vocabulary, our framework 69.8 +2.2 71.0 64.9 77.8 78.3 57.1

The sampled token is the weak signal, and unevenly so. It still gains 1.7 on STEM and 1.1 on GUI, but VQA does not move (0.0) and OCR falls by 1.0, whereas the three distribution-level objectives gain 2.0 to 2.7 on VQA and 0.2 to 0.8 on OCR. One reading is that a sampled token says which answer the teacher scored, while a distribution carries what else the teacher considered at every position, and the VQA and OCR domains lean on that more.

Truncation size barely matters. Top-16 and top-128 reach the same average with eight times the tokens, and the full-vocabulary loss ends close to both, so keeping more of the distribution buys no points once you supervise with one. The full-vocabulary loss has no truncation size to tune or revisit when the teachers or domains change. The usual reason to truncate is cost, and within verl it holds: 718 seconds per update for the full vocabulary against 522 for top-16. Our framework runs the same loss in 266 seconds (framework note).

In practice

  • Supervise with a distribution, not the sampled token.
  • Use full-vocabulary reverse KL as the default. If your target is knowledge-heavy VQA rather than precise outputs, test forward KL or ToDi; their lead showed in the first 30 to 40 updates.
  • Don’t spend time tuning k.
  • Compare divergences by benchmark scores at a fixed update count, never by loss curves.
